Key finding: Verizon hosts the largest wholesale book (~45M subs across cable MVNOs, Visible, and TracFone family) but captures the worst per-sub economics — cable MVNOs negotiated favorable wholesale rates in 2017. T-Mobile is vertically integrating (acquired Mint/Ultra 2024) to recapture retail margin. AT&T owns Cricket (~12M, largest single prepaid brand) and hosts the fewest third-party MVNOs.

Strategic Implications
Cable MVNOs are the structural threat: Spectrum Mobile (~11.5M) and Xfinity Mobile (~8.5M) added a combined ~1M subs in Q1 2026 — faster than any postpaid carrier. They use Verizon's network at favorable 2017-negotiated wholesale rates while undercutting Verizon retail by $30-50/line. Verizon hosts its own competitors.
T-Mobile's vertical integration playbook: Acquired Mint/Ultra for $1.35B (2024) to convert wholesale margin into retail margin. Combined with owned Metro (~21M subs), T-Mobile now captures the full economics on ~25M prepaid/MVNO lines — versus Verizon's TracFone book that's net-declining.
EchoStar is the wild card: Boost has ~7M subs, own 5G spectrum, and just sold AT&T $23B in unused spectrum (Aug 2025). EchoStar is strategically optional — could be acquired, could pivot to enterprise / satellite, or could continue as the only "4th carrier" attempt with its own retail brand.
