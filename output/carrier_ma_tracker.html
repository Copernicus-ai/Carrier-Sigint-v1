Strategic Themes
Fiber convergence dominates deal volume. Of $80B+ in tracked deals, ~$32B is fiber-related (VZ/Frontier $20B + ATT/Lumen $5.75B + TMUS JVs $5.85B). Every Tier-1 wireless carrier is now buying fiber to defend the household — the "convergence" thesis has moved from talking point to capital allocation.
EchoStar is the spectrum monetizer of the cycle. $40B+ in spectrum sales between the AT&T and SpaceX deals (Aug 2025). EchoStar pivots from "fourth carrier" ambitions to MVNO + asset-light model. The AT&T deal restocks AT&T's mid-band; the SpaceX deal seeds direct-to-cell satellite cellular.
T-Mobile is the most active strategic player. 5 deals in 18 months: UScellular wireless, UScellular spectrum, Lumos fiber JV, Metronet fiber JV, Mint MVNO acquisition. TMUS is executing the "convergence + scale" playbook end-to-end while VZ executes one big bet (Frontier) and AT&T plays defense.
Verizon's Frontier deal is the biggest single carrier bet of the cycle — and it closed Q1 2026. $20B all-cash for 7.2M fiber passings. VZ's consumer fiber base roughly doubles to ~12M subs, restoring fiber parity with AT&T and giving VZ the bundle ammo it needs against cable. Execution + integration is now the story for Verizon: turning the footprint into revenue.
May 2026: the spectrum reshuffle consummated. EchoStar's two big spectrum sales — $23B to AT&T and $17B to SpaceX — both cleared regulatory and closed in mid-May 2026. AT&T now sits on the largest mid-band capacity expansion of the cycle. SpaceX gets its direct-to-cell AWS-4 spectrum and establishes satellite as a real carrier spectrum-buyer category for the first time. EchoStar emerges with ~$40B in cash and pivots to MVNO + asset-light operations via Boost.
The old UScellular shell is a second, quieter spectrum monetizer. Array Digital Infrastructure — what's left of UScellular's parent after selling the wireless operating business to T-Mobile in Aug 2025 — spent May–June 2026 selling off the spectrum it kept: $168M to T-Mobile (May) and $1.0B to Verizon (Jun 1, FCC-approved May 14). Proceeds funded an $11.00/share special dividend to Array holders. Smaller than EchoStar's headline numbers, but the same underlying pattern: a legacy carrier exits the network business and re-emerges as a pure spectrum landlord.
Every tracked deal has now closed — the review backlog is empty. With AT&T/Lumen clearing Feb 2, 2026, there are no announced-but-pending deals left in this tracker for the first time since it started. Watch this space for the next wave: AT&T's Forged Fiber 37 stake sale is the most likely near-term new deal to appear here.
