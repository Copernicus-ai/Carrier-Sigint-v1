What am I looking at?

Each cell in the matrix is one YouTube ad. All the data lives in the corner badge in the upper-left of each thumbnail. The badge encodes three things at once:

  1. Top number ("157K", "2.3K", "365") = total views the ad has accumulated since posted.
  2. Bottom number ("13.1K/d", "11/d", "<1/d") = current views per day right now — our proxy for active paid amplification.
  3. Background color = velocity tier: 🟢 green ≥100 v/day (actively amplified, paid push behind it), 🟠 amber 10–99 v/day (moderate), ⚫ slate <10 v/day (essentially organic — often archiver channels).

Which carrier owns the ad? Check the row label on the left (T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon, Xfinity, Spectrum). Each row is one carrier.

Click any thumbnail to watch the ad on YouTube.

Total views vs velocity tell different stories. Total views (top number) = how big the ad got over its lifetime — old ads have more total. Velocity (bottom number + tier color) = how fast it's still being pushed right now — a measure of current spend behind it.

An old ad can have a huge total but a low v/day (its peak passed). A new ad can have a small total but a high v/day (just launched, big media buy). Example reading: a T-Mobile ad posted October 2025 with the badge showing "2.3K / 11/d" on an amber background means: 2,300 total views since October, currently still getting ~11 views/day, which puts it in the amber tier (10–99 v/d).

T-Mobile caveat: most T-Mobile iPhone YouTube data comes from archiver channels (third-party uploaders), not T-Mobile's official channel. T-Mobile barely uploads iPhone ads to YouTube — their iPhone push is TV-only. T-Mobile v/day numbers systematically understate true reach.

Carry-forward cells (faded thumbnail with "↪ cont." badge) mark months where an ad from the prior month was still running, even though it wasn't re-uploaded to YouTube that month. The matrix shows YouTube upload date, but TV campaigns typically run for 1–3 months; carry-forwards make that continuity visible. Click the carry-forward to jump to the source ad.

TV-only cells (amber bordered "📺 TV-only" boxes) mark months where iSpot confirms the carrier aired an ad but no YouTube upload exists for that creative. Example: Verizon's "Mother's Day Hockey" iPhone 17 spot ran 1,498 times on TV in May 2026 per iSpot, but Verizon did not upload it to YouTube.
