SIGINT — Cable MVNO Net Adds, Q2 2026

Xfinity Mobile (Comcast) and Spectrum Mobile (Charter) quarterly wireless line trends, Q2 2025 vs Q2 2026. Both ride Verizon’s network as MVNOs — they don’t disclose device cost-of-sales the way facilities-based carriers do, so this tracks line growth and wireless revenue rather than subsidy economics (see The Promotion Game for that view on AT&T / T-Mobile / Verizon).

Source: SEC EDGAR 8-K Q2 2026 (Comcast filed Jul 23; Charter filed Jul 24, 2026)
Key finding: The two cable MVNOs are diverging. Comcast’s Xfinity Mobile net adds accelerated year-over-year (378K → 448K, +18.5%) even as its core broadband business keeps shrinking — wireless is now the growth engine. Charter’s Spectrum Mobile net adds decelerated (491K → 406K, −17.3%) despite still being the larger book by total lines, suggesting earlier entry is running into saturation of its own broadband base.

Charter's release cites "higher mobile device revenue" as a cost driver but doesn't disclose a device revenue or cost dollar figure.
Why no subsidy-economics panel: AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile each disclose equipment revenue and cost of equipment sold, letting us compute a device-subsidy loss and burn rate (see The Promotion Game). Comcast discloses wireless equipment revenue but not its cost; Charter discloses neither separately. Consolidated EBITDA/FCF for both companies also span their whole cable business (video, broadband, and — for Comcast — NBCUniversal/Peacock), not wireless alone, so mixing those into the carrier subsidy-burn-rate chart would compare fundamentally different things. This page instead tracks the metrics both companies actually disclose for their wireless arms: line growth and service revenue.
