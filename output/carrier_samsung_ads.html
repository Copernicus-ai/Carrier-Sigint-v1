What am I looking at?

Each cell in the matrix is one YouTube ad. Three visual elements per ad encode three different signals:

1. The badge (upper-left corner of the thumbnail) — two things at once:

  • The badge text ("330K", "70M", "3") = total views the ad has accumulated since posted.
  • The badge background color = velocity tier: 🟢 green ≥100 v/day, 🟠 amber 10–99 v/day, ⚫ slate <10 v/day.

2. The bar (thin colored strip directly below the thumbnail) — also two things at once:

  • Bar color = which carrier owns the ad: T-Mobile pink, AT&T blue, Verizon red, Xfinity purple, Spectrum blue. Matches the row label on the left.
  • Bar height = how fast the ad is still getting views per day right now — proxy for active paid amplification.

3. The thumbnail image = the ad creative itself. Click to watch on YouTube.

Why the bar height uses a log scale: the range between ads is enormous — biggest is ~336,000 v/day (Verizon "A Better Deal"), smallest is ~3 v/day. That's a 100,000× spread. On a linear bar chart, every ad except the biggest would look invisible. So each step up in bar height means 10× more daily views — like a Richter scale.

Bar heightRoughly means
tiny (~2px)a few views/day · basically organic noise
short (~3–4px)~10 views/day
medium (~5px)~100 views/day
tall (~7px)~1,000 views/day
maxed (~9px)10,000+ views/day · heavy paid push

A 5-pixel bar isn't "a bit more than" a 3-pixel bar — it's 100× more daily activity.

Why Samsung is so sparse: T-Mobile runs huge Samsung TV campaigns (3,179 airings of "Scales") but uploads almost nothing to YouTube. Verizon and AT&T have similar gaps. The empty cells are the intelligence finding — carrier Samsung co-op is structurally TV-first.

The Verizon "A Better Deal" anomaly: the 70M-view amber-outlined ad is a Verizon brand campaign, not a Samsung device co-op. It surfaced in our Samsung search because Samsung phones appear briefly in the spot. Included for market context, flagged with amber outline.
