Carrier Ad Intelligence:
iPhone vs. Samsung, TV vs. YouTube

Coverage: Sep 2025 – May 2026  ·  Carriers: T-Mobile · AT&T · Verizon · Xfinity Mobile · Spectrum Mobile  ·  Sources: YouTube Data API v3 · iSpot.tv brand pages · SEC 8-K filings · Carrier IR releases · YouTube archiver channels · abancommercials.com  ·  Published: May 2026
Headline Finding
YouTube data tells the inverse of TV reality for Samsung.
T-Mobile is the largest Samsung TV advertiser in the country — and completely invisible on YouTube.
AT&T leads iPhone co-op on both channels. T-Mobile runs 3,179 Samsung TV spots with zero official YouTube uploads. Verizon buys Samsung, iPhone, and Google Pixel simultaneously — the only truly device-agnostic carrier. Samsung's own YouTube channel (54M views on a single launch ad) dwarfs all carrier co-op combined.

Methodology · Why Two Channels

YouTube and TV Tell Different Stories

This analysis combines two data streams that carriers treat as distinct spend buckets. YouTube view velocity (views/day) proxies for paid digital amplification — how much a carrier invests in boosting a specific creative on YouTube. TV airing counts from iSpot.tv represent traditional broadcast and cable placements. Neither channel alone gives the full picture.

The core distortion: T-Mobile uploads almost no advertising content to YouTube. Their TV campaigns — including their largest Samsung co-op — exist only as broadcast. A YouTube-only analysis concludes T-Mobile barely advertises Samsung. The TV record shows the opposite: they're the category's biggest Samsung spender.

AT&T reads as the most disciplined: iPhone-first across both channels, consistent creative cadence Sep–May, 294K Q1 phone net adds. Their Festival ad ran 1,500 TV airings AND generated 141K YouTube views — the only carrier executing true cross-channel amplification on a single creative.

Every view on their iPhone ads comes from archiver channels (max 65 v/d Verizon, 47 v/d T-Mobile) — unamplifed, organic noise.

Methodology & Caveats

What This Analysis Cannot Tell You

  • YouTube view velocity is a proxy for paid amplification, not a direct spend measurement. A high v/day suggests media buying behind the creative; it does not confirm a specific dollar figure.
  • T-Mobile's YouTube figures are from archiver channels, not official carrier uploads. All T-Mobile YouTube velocity numbers should be treated as lower-bound estimates of true reach.
  • iSpot.tv airing counts reflect national placements tracked by iSpot's panel. Local and regional buys may not be fully captured. The data window for each carrier may differ slightly.
  • Verizon "A Better Deal" (70M YouTube views) is a brand campaign, not a Samsung device co-op. It is included in the Samsung tracker for market context but should not be cited as Samsung co-op spend.
  • Net subscriber adds are influenced by pricing, network quality, promotions, device launch timing, and competitive actions — not advertising alone. Do not attribute subscriber outcomes to ad campaigns.
  • Xfinity Mobile and Spectrum Mobile do not report standalone phone net adds in their public filings. Their iSpot data was not retrieved for this brief.
  • Samsung's own YouTube channel figures (54M, 30M, 24M views) represent Samsung's direct-to-consumer media investment, not carrier co-op. Apple has no equivalent direct YouTube strategy.
