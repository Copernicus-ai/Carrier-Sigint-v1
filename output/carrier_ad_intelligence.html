Carrier Ad Intelligence:
iPhone vs. Samsung, TV vs. YouTube

Coverage: Sep 2025 – Aug 2026  ·  Carriers: T-Mobile · AT&T · Verizon · Xfinity Mobile · Spectrum Mobile · Cricket Wireless  ·  Sources: YouTube Data API v3 · iSpot.tv brand pages · SEC 8-K filings · Carrier IR releases · YouTube archiver channels · abancommercials.com  ·  Published: May 2026  ·  Updated: Aug 2026 — added Cricket Wireless
Headline Finding
YouTube data tells the inverse of TV reality for Samsung.
T-Mobile is the largest Samsung TV advertiser in the country — and completely invisible on YouTube.
AT&T leads iPhone co-op on both channels. T-Mobile runs 3,179 Samsung TV spots with zero official YouTube uploads. Verizon buys Samsung, iPhone, and Google Pixel simultaneously — the only truly device-agnostic carrier. Both Apple and Samsung run heavy direct YouTube channels: Apple's "Compare iPhone Lineup" (80M views, 318K v/d) is the single most-watched iPhone 17 ad anywhere; Samsung's "Devil Wears Prada 2" runs at 1.17M v/day.

Methodology · Why Two Channels

YouTube and TV Tell Different Stories

This analysis combines two data streams that carriers treat as distinct spend buckets. YouTube view velocity (views/day) proxies for paid digital amplification — how much a carrier invests in boosting a specific creative on YouTube. TV airing counts from iSpot.tv represent traditional broadcast and cable placements. Neither channel alone gives the full picture.

The core distortion: T-Mobile uploads almost no advertising content to YouTube. Their TV campaigns — including their largest Samsung co-op — exist only as broadcast. A YouTube-only analysis concludes T-Mobile barely advertises Samsung. The TV record shows the opposite: they're the category's biggest Samsung spender.
⚠ Important framing — "carrier co-op," not manufacturer direct. All figures in this brief measure carrier co-op TV — ads funded by carriers (T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon) that feature a specific device. They do not include Apple's or Samsung's direct manufacturer TV ads. Total carrier co-op TV airings tracked: ~6,937 Samsung vs ~3,174 iPhone — Samsung wins by ~2.2× and that result is almost entirely T-Mobile's doing (6,033 Samsung airings vs 21 iPhone). This counter-intuitive finding does not mean Samsung outspends Apple on TV globally — Apple's direct TV ad spend is among the largest in the U.S., far exceeding Samsung Mobile's direct spend. The signal here is that Samsung effectively buys carrier distribution by subsidizing carrier co-op (especially TMUS), while Apple relies on being the default consumer choice plus its own direct media.

AT&T reads as the most disciplined: iPhone-first across both channels, consistent creative cadence Sep–May, 294K Q1 phone net adds. Their Festival ad ran 1,500 TV airings AND generated 141K YouTube views — the only carrier executing true cross-channel amplification on a single creative.
T-Mobile's YouTube invisibility is a strategic choice, not an oversight: They upload almost nothing to YouTube. Their 14,519 TV airings (Spend Rank #8) and 3,179 Samsung "Scales" spots are pure broadcast. YouTube analytics would never surface this. Across all 43 tracked iPhone 17 carrier creatives (Sep 2025–May 2026), T-Mobile and Verizon have zero official YouTube uploads. Every view on their iPhone ads comes from archiver channels (max 65 v/d Verizon, 47 v/d T-Mobile) — unamplifed, organic noise.
Case study — Cricket Wireless (AT&T's prepaid sub-brand, added Aug 2026): Not a full profile card — Cricket doesn't report standalone subscriber/financial metrics (folded into AT&T's consolidated prepaid segment), so it isn't directly comparable to the three postpaid carriers above. But its ad pattern is a clean data point for the brief's core theme: device marketing here is overwhelmingly Samsung/Motorola (6,727 national TV airings in the current 30-day iSpot window, led by Samsung Galaxy A-series deals), with iPhone appearing only as a discounted iPhone 16/16e trade-down — never the current iPhone 17 generation. This is the inverse of AT&T's own postpaid strategy (iPhone-first) one tier up the same corporate family, and a starker version of the Samsung-buys-distribution pattern seen at T-Mobile.

Methodology & Caveats

What This Analysis Cannot Tell You

  • YouTube view velocity is a proxy for paid amplification, not a direct spend measurement. A high v/day suggests media buying behind the creative; it does not confirm a specific dollar figure.
  • T-Mobile's YouTube figures are from archiver channels, not official carrier uploads. All T-Mobile YouTube velocity numbers should be treated as lower-bound estimates of true reach.
  • iSpot.tv airing counts reflect national placements tracked by iSpot's panel. Local and regional buys may not be fully captured. The data window for each carrier may differ slightly.
  • ¶ Per-creative airing counts are NOT carrier totals. The "21 (Go Grande)", "1,500 (Music Festival)", and "1,498 (Mother's Day Hockey)" figures are airings of a single named creative that iSpot's brand-summary page surfaced. They are not "T-Mobile's total iPhone TV airings" or "AT&T's total iPhone TV airings." Carriers run iPhone in brand/switch creative that isn't iSpot-tagged as iPhone-specific. Use these as directional comparisons between named creatives, not as carrier-level iPhone TV totals. T-Mobile's 14,519 brand total includes everything; only the 21 was iSpot-classified as a dedicated iPhone spot.
  • Verizon "A Better Deal" (70M YouTube views) is a brand campaign, not a Samsung device co-op. It is included in the Samsung tracker for market context but should not be cited as Samsung co-op spend.
    Why does Samsung appear in it but not iPhone? Apple maintains strict brand control over iPhone's media presence: any carrier-funded ad featuring iPhone requires explicit co-op approval and iPhone-focused creative. Apple does not allow iPhone to be used as incidental visual scenery in generic carrier brand ads. Samsung's posture is the opposite — they are happy to have Samsung phones shown in carrier brand spots as props at no cost, because it's free brand impressions. So when Verizon needed a phone on-screen for "A Better Deal," Samsung filled the slot. This is consistent with the broader theme of the brief: Apple relies on consumer default; Samsung pays (or is willingly free) for carrier distribution.
  • Net subscriber adds are influenced by pricing, network quality, promotions, device launch timing, and competitive actions — not advertising alone. Do not attribute subscriber outcomes to ad campaigns.
  • Xfinity Mobile and Spectrum Mobile do not report standalone phone net adds in their public filings. Their iSpot data was not retrieved for this brief.
  • Cricket Wireless is AT&T's prepaid MVNO brand, not a standalone carrier — it reports no separate subscriber/net-add figures, so it is excluded from the Q1 2026 financial-context table and profile-card grid above. Its iSpot brand-page capture (Aug 2026) is a different, more recent snapshot window than the May 2026 capture used for the other three carriers' TV tables, so raw totals should not be compared carrier-to-carrier; only the creative-mix pattern (Samsung-heavy, zero iPhone 17) is the intended signal. Cricket's Samsung YouTube velocity was not pulled for this update — only its iPhone 16e figure.
  • Both manufacturer-direct YouTube figures (Apple Direct top: 80M / 30M / 24M views; Samsung Direct top: 54M / 30M / 24M views) represent direct-to-consumer media investment, not carrier co-op. Apple's direct YouTube footprint is larger by content volume (25+ iPhone 17 videos since Sep 2025); Samsung pushes specific viral creative (Devil Wears Prada 2 at 1.17M v/day) more aggressively per video. An earlier draft of this brief understated Apple's direct YouTube presence — that has been corrected.
